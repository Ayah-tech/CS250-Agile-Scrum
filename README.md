# CS250 - Agile Scrum

A portfolio project showcasing Agile and Scrum practices, sprint reviews, retrospectives, and software development lifecycle concepts learned in CS-250 at Southern New Hampshire University.

## Project Overview

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective from the SNHU Travel project. Throughout this course, I explored how Scrum roles, user stories, sprint planning, communication, and feedback support software development.

## Understanding User Needs and User Stories

I interpret user needs by understanding what users want to accomplish and translating those needs into clear requirements. User stories help me focus on the user's perspective instead of only thinking about technical features. During the SNHU Travel project, I created user stories for a travel application, including a feature that displays personalized travel deals. Writing acceptance criteria helped clarify what the feature should do and how to determine whether it meets the user's expectations.

## My Approach to Software Development

I approach developing programs by understanding the problem, breaking it into smaller tasks, and testing my work as I make progress. This course helped me understand the importance of planning, communication, and adapting to changes. In future projects, I hope to incorporate Agile practices such as maintaining a product backlog, sprint planning, regular progress updates, sprint reviews, and retrospectives. These practices can help me stay organized, respond to feedback, and continuously improve my work.

## Being a Good Team Member

I believe being a good team member in software development means communicating clearly, respecting different perspectives, and taking responsibility for assigned work. Through the Scrum activities in this course, I learned how the Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers contribute to a shared goal. Although these activities were simulations, they helped me understand the importance of asking questions, identifying obstacles early, and supporting other team members. I believe collaboration and a willingness to learn are essential for building successful software.
